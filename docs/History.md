# Blue Water Riptide — Lore & World

[🇺🇸 English](History.md) · [🇧🇷 Português](History.pt-BR.md)

Narrative/flavor doc only. It gives players a reason to care about a Sailor or squadron before picking one — it does not add, change, or imply any new mechanics, stats, modes, or content scope. **v1 remains exactly 2 playable Sailors (Ensign Ace, Admiral Anchor) and 1 arena (Tideline Cove)**; everything else here is flavor for the roadmap roster named in [`.claude/plans/00-game-design-overview.md`](../.claude/plans/00-game-design-overview.md) §4, written so it's ready to drop in when those Sailors actually ship. As with the rest of the plans: the marine/navy/volleyball theme is cosmetic. Knockouts are competitive-arena knockouts — a Sailor who's "out" spectates the rest of the round, nothing worse. No ball, no scoring zones, no real warfare.

## 1. World premise: the Tideline Cup

Every year, right before the regional fleets scatter for the long deployment season, every ship that can spare a crew anchors at Tideline Cove for one last stop: the **Tideline Cup**. It's officially a "shakedown exercise" on the paperwork. Unofficially, it's the only thing anyone on any of these ships has been talking about for weeks.

The story sailors tell each other (embellished a little more every year) is that it started as a dockside argument between two crews over whose gig-boat was faster, which was about to turn into an actual brawl on the pier — until an old harbor officer stepped between them, dragged a volleyball net out of storage, and told them to settle it *there* instead. It worked well enough that the admiralty made it official: a friendly, fiercely competitive tournament, fought in teams, arena-style, with a clear and harmless win condition and zero appetite for anyone actually getting hurt. The knockout format that came out of it is still called **Sink or Swim** — you're either standing at the end of the round, or you've been tagged out and you're watching from the sideline benches, dripping and heckling your teammates.

The trophy is a barnacle-crusted ship's bell nobody can agree the real origin of (three different squadrons claim it came off *their* founding vessel). Whoever's squadron holds the most Cup wins for the season gets to keep it on display until the next Cup — and gets bragging rights over every other crew in port until then, which matters a lot more than the bell itself.

Tideline Cove is where the Cup has always been held — it's shallow, sheltered, and every fleet in the region can reach it inside a day's sail. As the Cup grows, it's understood (in-world) that the format could move to other coves and courts around the region — which is exactly why the game's arena system is built to add more courts later without changing anything about how a match works.

This is the whole reason three very different squadrons keep ending up on the same beach, net up, ready to go: it's not war, it's not a job, it's the one event all year where everybody agrees to settle it as a game.

## 2. The three squadrons

### The Blue Water Squad — "Do it right, or do it again"

Blue Water is the by-the-book fleet: proper drills, proper chain of command, proper uniforms even on the beach. They were the ones who formalized the Cup in the first place, and they've never quite let anyone forget it — as far as Blue Water is concerned, they're not just playing the tournament, they're the reason it exists and the standard everyone else is held to.

That doesn't make them boring to play. Blue Water's whole identity is *versatility done well* — every kit in the squad is built to be sound in more situations than it's flashy in any one of them. They respect the Anchor Guard as fellow professionals (mutual nods across the net, no bad blood) and view the Riptide Rush as talented but undisciplined — which the Rush finds hilarious, because Blue Water loses to them constantly and still can't stop being smug about form.

**Why main them**: you like a kit that rewards good decisions over flashy ones — positioning, timing, controlling space — and you want a squad that's competent at literally everything rather than spiky at one thing.

### The Riptide Rush — "Fast enough, you don't need a plan"

The Rush is the youngest squadron on the circuit, made up mostly of small-boat crews — scouts, couriers, harbor-patrol tenders — who weren't even invited to the first few Cups and crashed the tournament anyway. They still tell that story like it's their founding myth, because it is. Where Blue Water drills, the Rush just shows up fast, hits hard, and argues about tactics *after* the round, usually while celebrating.

They're loud, a little reckless, and genuinely having more fun than anyone else at the Cup, which is most of their appeal to a crowd. Their rivalry with Blue Water Squad is the marquee matchup of the season — order versus chaos, and chaos wins often enough to keep it interesting. With the Anchor Guard they mostly keep a wary, good-natured distance: the Rush respects that Anchor Guard actually stops them, which is rarer than the Rush would like.

**Why main them**: you want to be the one making things happen every second of the match — high tempo, high risk, and a squad that turns a losing position around through sheer aggression rather than patience.

### The Anchor Guard — "Nothing gets past us"

The Anchor Guard grew out of the harbor-defense crews and lighthouse keepers who used to just referee and host the Cup from the shore before deciding they'd rather compete. They're older on average, less interested in medals, and treat "holding a line" as a point of pride the way Blue Water treats protocol. If you break through an Anchor Guard formation, you earned it — they will not make it easy, and they will absolutely bring it up if you win by a fluke.

They're on friendly, respectful terms with Blue Water (two disciplined outfits recognizing each other) but they're fiercely independent — the Guard doesn't answer to fleet command the way the others do, and they like it that way. To the Rush, the Guard is the wall that ruins their fun, and the Guard is completely fine with that reputation.

**Why main them**: you want to be the immovable object — soak pressure, own a chokepoint, and win matches by outlasting people rather than outrunning them.

## 3. Per-character hooks

Two of these Sailors (Ensign Ace, Admiral Anchor) are live in v1. The rest are roadmap content from [`.claude/plans/00-game-design-overview.md`](../.claude/plans/00-game-design-overview.md) §4 — not currently playable — written here so their personalities are locked in ahead of time.

### The Blue Water Squad

**Ensign Ace** *(Starter · Serve)* — The Squad's youngest officer and, loudly, its top scorer. Ace treats every match like his shot at proving the brass promoted him early for a reason, which means he's constantly a half-step ahead of his own confidence. He's the Sailor every new player learns the Cup through: keep your distance, keep firing, keep moving — and every knockout he lands gets him moving that little bit faster, because Ace plays best on a streak.

**Captain Cove** *(Blue Water controller — wall-spawning `Block` Super)* — The Squad's actual tactician, and the person who unofficially runs Ace's whole career. Cove doesn't chase kills; Cove decides where the fight happens, plants a line, and makes everyone else fight on Blue Water's terms. Calm to the point of unsettling opponents who expected a navy officer to be more excitable.

**Sailor Smash** *(Blue Water bruiser)* — Started as ship's cargo crew, built the kind of strength that comes from years of manual labor, and hit a serve so hard in his first scrimmage that the referees made him try out for the Squad on the spot. No finesse, no apologies — Smash wins arguments about subtlety by not having them.

**Deep Blue** *(first Legendary — delayed-strike `Lob` artillery)* — The most senior Sailor in the Squad and the only one nobody's ever seen rattled. Deep Blue plays patient, almost slow, right up until a lobbed strike lands out of nowhere and the match tips. The rumor mill says Deep Blue served on something bigger than a patrol boat once and doesn't talk about it; the Squad lets that rumor stand because it's good for morale.

### The Riptide Rush

**Riptide Rookie** *(Common skirmisher)* — The newest face on the fastest squad, and thrilled about it. Rookie has more energy than technique and makes up the gap by never, ever slowing down — the kind of Sailor who treats "reckless" as a compliment and is trying very hard to make the veterans on the Rush proud.

**Surge Serve** *(Rush marksman)* — The one Rush Sailor who actually aims before firing, which the rest of the squad rides him about constantly. Surge grew up on the smallest, fastest boats in the fleet and never lost the instinct to hit a moving target from a moving platform — precision at speed is his whole trick, and it works.

**Foam Flier** *(Rush assassin)* — In, tag someone out, gone before the other team's even turned around. Flier treats the Cup like a highlight reel and plays for the crowd as much as the win — the kind of Sailor who'll take a flashier route around the flank purely because it looks better on the replay.

**Stormy Spike** *(Rush ramping damage)* — Moody, competitive, and gets visibly more dangerous the longer a fight drags on — teammates joke that Spike brings his own weather. Feeds off crowd noise and a close scoreline; the worst thing an opponent can do is let a match against Spike go long.

### The Anchor Guard

**Admiral Anchor** *(Rare · Spike)* — The Guard's senior commander and the closest thing the whole Cup has to an elder statesman. Anchor doesn't chase the fight, he makes the fight come to him, and once he's planted somewhere the smart move is to go around, not through. Mentors half the younger Sailors on the circuit whether they asked for it or not, Rush and Blue Water included.

**Tide Turner** *(Common support — first `Set`/healer)* — The Guard's quartermaster-turned-medic, equal parts patch-up crew and hype squad. Tide Turner can flip a losing fight back in the Guard's favor just by keeping everyone standing a few seconds longer than the other team expects — the squad's quiet MVP more often than the scoreboard shows.

**Coral Claw** *(Anchor Guard defensive specialist)* — A salvage diver by trade, comfortable working reef and rock long before the Cup existed, and just as comfortable holding a flank now. Claw's reputation is simple: pick a spot, dig in, and make anyone who tries to pass through it regret the attempt.

## 4. The hook

Once a year, every crew in the fleet gets one shot to settle who's actually the best — not with orders, with a net, a beach, and a scoreboard. Pick your squadron: drill with the disciplined Blue Water Squad, crash the party with the reckless Riptide Rush, or dig in and dare anyone to get past the Anchor Guard. Everybody's got a reason to win the Cup. What's yours?
